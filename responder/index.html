The Responder

A missed call is a customer who rings someone else.

You were up a ladder, under a sink, or with another customer. By the time you hear the voicemail they have already called the next name on the list. The Responder texts them back within seconds, so the job stays yours.

It works both ways. Miss the call and they get a text saying you will ring back. Take the call and they still get one — you dictate it as you hang up, and they get “Tuesday 9am, see you then” in writing while they are still holding the phone.

$300 to set it up, $250 a month to run it. One job, done properly, and you can switch it off whenever you like.

This one is not a button. Setting it up means your number, your forwarding, and words written for your business — so it starts with a conversation and I set it up with you.

The complete missed-call flow

One missed call. Five clear moves.

There is nothing to pick or configure on this page. This is the whole flow at once; your actual wording and boundaries are written and tested with you during setup.

  1. 01

    Call missed

    The customer calls your normal business number. With the agreed call path in place, an unanswered call starts only the response you approved.

  2. 02

    Instant acknowledgement

    A short text goes out within seconds. It says who they reached, acknowledges that the call was missed, and tells them a person will follow up. It never pretends a human answered.

  3. 03

    Useful, bounded help

    It can ask only the questions agreed during setup — such as their name, the kind of job, service area, or urgency — and can give checked basics such as business hours.

    It does not invent a quote, book a time, or promise work unless that exact action is part of the written scope.

  4. 04

    Human handoff

    The reply and useful context go to the person you named. You follow up as the business owner; anything unusual, urgent, or outside the approved words stops and waits for a person.

  5. 05

    Confirmed close or stop

    The exchange ends when the next step is confirmed, the customer asks it to stop, or an agreed boundary is reached. It does not keep chasing someone after the job is handed off or the conversation is closed.
