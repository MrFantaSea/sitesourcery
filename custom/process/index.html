Custom process

From scattered facts to one deliberate world.

The process protects both the idea and the details: understand the business, map the actual job, choose a visual direction, build the experience, and verify that the delivered site matches the written scope.

Start with a conversation Jump to scope binding

The working sequence

  1. 01

    Discover the truth

    We begin with the business as it is: audiences, services, locations, priorities, public claims, present site, useful tools, frustrations, and desired outcome.

  2. 02

    Map the whole job

    Existing routes and content are inventoried. Required pages, layouts, media, systems, data, and transition work are separated so nothing important hides inside a shorthand package.

  3. 03

    Set the direction

    We choose Essential, Distinctive, or Atelier according to the experience you want—not according to how many pages the site contains.

  4. 04

    Design the system

    Information architecture, content hierarchy, art direction, responsive behavior, reusable components, and important visitor paths are developed as one connected design.

  5. 05

    Build and refine

    The real pages are implemented, reviewed, and revised against the agreed direction. Interaction remains purposeful and the experience remains usable without depending on spectacle.

  6. 06

    Verify the handoff

    Routes, links, responsive layouts, accessibility fundamentals, content coverage, integrations, redirects, and written boundaries are checked before an authorized release.

Scope binding

The idea, the quote, and the build stay the same project.

A beautiful concept is not useful if the written scope describes less work—or quietly promises more.

The route plan, visible sections, distinct layouts, creative direction, systems and data, migration assumptions, and explicit exclusions are reconciled before the personalized written quote. Changes remain named rather than smuggled into the work.

  • Route coverage
  • Content responsibility
  • Creative direction
  • System boundaries
  • Migration decisions
  • Verification

Responsibilities and checkpoints

Every phase has an owner and an exit.

The project schedule names real dates only after the accepted scope, starting payment, complete materials, access, and decision-maker are in place. Time waiting on missing input is visible and can move the release window.

01

Fit and intake

Establish the source of truth

You provide
Business facts, present-site context, goals, constraints, decision-maker, materials, and known account access.
Studio owns
Discovery questions, evidence review, gaps, risks, and a first map of the actual job.
Phase exits with
A discovery record that distinguishes known facts, open questions, and assumptions.
02

Scope and acceptance

Turn the map into one written deal

You provide
Corrections, final priorities, required materials, and acceptance of the personalized quote and matching terms.
Studio owns
Footprint counts, creative boundary, systems, transition, exclusions, responsibilities, schedule mechanics, and payment timing.
Phase exits with
An accepted scope receipt, required starting payment, complete inputs, and a dated production window.
03

Direction approval

Choose the visual world before scaling it

You provide
One consolidated response from the named decision-maker by the review date.
Studio owns
The chosen Essential, Distinctive, or Atelier direction and representative compositions at the agreed fidelity.
Phase exits with
A recorded direction approval or one grouped refinement round within the quoted boundary.
04

Production and review

Build the real routes and systems

You provide
Final approved copy and media, access needed for scoped connections, and consolidated feedback in each included round.
Studio owns
Responsive implementation, accessibility fundamentals, content placement, scoped connections, and a reviewable release candidate.
Phase exits with
Every quoted route and component accounted for; unresolved changes are either corrected or named as new scope.
05

Release and cutover

Move deliberately, with a way back

You provide
Final content approval, release authorization, and the required owner-controlled domain or provider access.
Studio owns
Preflight checks, backups where applicable, redirect and record plan, release sequence, verification, and named rollback boundary.
Phase exits with
The authorized site at its agreed destination or a documented rollback to the last known-good state.
06

Closeout and care

Leave ownership and next actions clear

You receive
The agreed source or export, ownership/access inventory, route and redirect record, scoped operating notes, and final verification record.
Studio closes
Final payment and ownership conditions, remaining credentials or access handoff, open exceptions, and any quoted correction window.
After closeout
Ongoing edits, hosting, monitoring, or care exist only when the written quote or a separately accepted care scope includes them.

Review and change mechanics

Feedback refines the approved job. New work changes it.

  • One list per round.The named decision-maker returns one consolidated set of comments by the review date.
  • Included refinement.An included round adjusts the approved direction, content placement, or implementation within the written footprint.
  • Written scope change.New routes, capabilities, content, direction, or restructuring receive a visible effect on scope, timing, and commercial terms before work continues.
  • Clock truth.The dated schedule begins from complete inputs. Waiting on materials, access, approval, or payment pauses the affected work and can move the release window.
