Ordinary public pages

The marketing pages do not use an inquiry form, visitor upload, advertising tracker, or page-level analytics code.

Read public-page details

Telephone and email links hand control to the application chosen on the visitor’s device. The infrastructure that delivers a page can still process ordinary request records described below.

Accounts and sessions

The service stores the account details and protected credentials needed to sign in and recover access.

Read account details

Account records can include the person’s name, organization, email address, credential digest, session identifiers, recovery-token digest and expiry, accepted document versions, account state, and security timestamps. Site Sourcery does not store the readable password. Recovery messages go through the transactional-message provider identified in current service disclosures.

Projects, drafts, and releases

The hosted service stores the facts and version records needed to save, review, publish, roll back, and export one Spark project.

Read project details

A project can include its name, organization, business facts, selected look, draft revisions, generated HTML, content and artifact digests, screening results, reviewed and accepted versions, publication history, access choice, address records, tenure and entitlement state, domain records, support records, safety events, and lifecycle timestamps. The released product contract is abracadabra.spark/v1, a one-page site; the service does not include migration, outside integrations, or human revision rounds.

Project data is stored on the Site Sourcery application and database infrastructure and on bounded backup or recovery systems used for this service. Authorized infrastructure providers process it only to deliver, protect, back up, restore, or monitor the service under their applicable agreements.

Public and access-controlled serving

A paid eligible project can serve only the accepted version at its verified address.

Read serving details

A public release is reachable by people who know or discover its address. A private release requires its project passphrase, represented by a protected digest rather than readable text. Access control reduces casual visibility but is not a vault. Do not place passwords, full payment-card data, health information, regulated records, or sensitive customer data in a project. Serving records can include hostname, certificate, release digest, access mode, status, timestamps, and request or abuse signals.

Hive planner

Hive remains a local planning demonstration and does not activate outside services.

Read Hive details

Hive uses fixed planning blueprints in the downloaded page. It does not send a message, make a booking, request a review, change an invoice, connect an outside service, or store the visitor’s planner choice in the hosted account.

Infrastructure providers and network records

Service infrastructure processes ordinary technical records needed to route, secure, and diagnose requests.

Read provider details

Application, database, backup, email, payment, domain, DNS, certificate, and network providers can process the records needed for their assigned role. Ordinary network records can include IP address, requested host and path, timestamp, response status, user-agent information, request identifier, rate-limit state, and security events. Site Sourcery does not sell customer information or use hosted project content for behavioral advertising.

Domains and registrant information

A customer-owned domain stays in the customer’s name, and its registrar needs accurate contact information.

Read domain details

Domain search, registration, renewal, transfer, and connection can require the requested name, availability and price responses, registrant and contact data, accepted registrar terms, authorization records, provider operation IDs, receipts, DNS records, verification challenges, renewal state, and timestamps. Site Sourcery identifies the underlying registrar before the customer confirms registration. The customer is the registrant; Site Sourcery does not claim ownership of a customer-owned domain.

Quotes and payment records

Card entry belongs to the disclosed payment processor; Site Sourcery keeps the quote, transaction reference, and status it needs.

Read payment details

The server creates an expiring quote from the approved catalog and the project’s current address choice. Secure card entry occurs with the payment processor identified at checkout. Site Sourcery can receive and store customer, checkout, payment, refund, dispute, invoice, and subscription references; quoted line items and totals; tenure; document versions; status; and timestamps. Site Sourcery does not need or request the full card number or security code. A held or disabled checkout creates no charge.

Retention, export, and deletion

Exports are time-limited; cancellation or suspension starts a 90-day project-retention period unless the customer deletes sooner.

Read retention details

A prepared export can contain project facts, drafts, complete version history, self-contained release files, address and publication history, support history, project state, and a receipt. Its authenticated download token is short-lived and one-time. The customer can prepare a new export after a token expires or is used.

Cancellation stops later renewals and begins the applicable 90-day retained exit period. An eligible recurring site keeps serving for 14 days after a missed payment and is suspended on day 15; project content and export remain retained for 90 days after suspension before deletion. A customer can request earlier terminal project deletion. Deletion removes project content, drafts, releases, serving credentials, domain-proof material, support narratives, and safety narratives, while minimal transaction, accepted-document, security, fraud-prevention, legal, and text-free lifecycle records may remain as required. Backups age out under the bounded backup schedule rather than being rewritten instantly.

Support, safety, and abuse handling

Support and safety records are used to answer requests, investigate reports, give notice, decide appeals, and protect the service.

Read support and safety details

Support records can include subject, message, project and account identifiers, status, response, and timestamps. Safety records can include a report, evidence reference, affected release, decision, notice, appeal, restoration or removal result, reviewer identifier, and timestamps. Access is limited to people and systems that need those records for support, security, abuse prevention, legal compliance, notice, or appeal.

Security and children

No system is perfectly secure, and these business services are not made for children under 13.

Read security details

Site Sourcery uses tenant boundaries, protected credentials, encrypted transport, request controls, version checks, bounded operator access, backups, audit records, and explicit lifecycle controls. No storage or transmission method is guaranteed to be perfectly secure. The service is directed to businesses and is not designed for children under 13.
