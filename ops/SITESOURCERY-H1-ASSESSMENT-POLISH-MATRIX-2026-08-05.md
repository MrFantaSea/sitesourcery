## 1. H1 outcome and authority

H1 is one complete vertical customer outcome:

anonymous inquiry → activated Site Sourcery account → customer-owned external
site asset → exact bounded $200 assessment quote → affirmative acceptance →
payable invoice → held-by-default Stripe Checkout → provider-confirmed payment
→ assessment job → up to ten findings with evidence → delivered account report
→ one eligible, one-use $200 Custom base-build credit.

- ops/SITESOURCERY-CUSTOM-SERVICES-COMMERCIAL-CONTRACT-2026-08-05.md:15-27
  for anonymous inquiry, account-before-pay, no free diagnosis, written scope,
  customer ownership, and no duplicate work;

### H1 is complete only when

- one anonymous inquiry can be received without inspection, payment, or an
  account;
- the same person can activate an account and claim or continue that inquiry
  without browser-supplied customer or organization authority;
- one external site asset is durably bound to that account and organization;
- the accepted quote names one business, one public website, one goal, up to
  five representative public pages or page types, desktop and phone review, up
  to ten findings with screenshot evidence, exclusions, $200 price, actual
  delivery date, credit terms, catalog/terms version, and expiry;
- no payable invoice or payment action exists before activated-account,
  current-site, and accepted-current-quote truth;
- a Checkout return or webhook never grants paid status by itself;
- provider readback plus one atomic local settlement creates one confirmed
  invoice/payment and one assessment job;
- the owner can complete the job from Mac or Pixel without reconstructing
  state from email or Stripe;
- delivery exposes one accessible report and atomically creates exactly one
  $200 eligible Custom base-build credit;
- duplicate commands, tabs, redirects, webhooks, delivery attempts, and
  report-notice retries cannot duplicate money, jobs, reports, or credits; and
- all public payment and timing promises remain held until the lead separately
  clears release.

### Explicitly outside H1

- expanded-assessment pricing or checkout;
- repairs, Rescue quote automation, selected-finding checkout, or automatic
  repair pricing;
- outside-site takeover, management, private admin access, or credentials;
- Custom build quote composition beyond proving that the delivered credit is
  eligible and one-use;
- Custom Care, Alakazam care, domains, Local Presence, Website Move, Responder,
  cancellation, refunds, disputes, or production release;
- an accessible downloadable PDF unless that separate artifact is proven; and
- any standard delivery, acknowledgement, or response-time promise.

## 3. One surface, six customer steps

The assessment journey belongs in a shared customer account dashboard. It may
reuse the existing authentication shell, but it must not be squeezed into the
Abracadabra four-step “Account / Project / $5 quote / Download” widget.

The H1 account rail is exactly:

1. Account
2. Website
3. Quote
4. Payment
5. Assessment
6. Report & credit

Anonymous inquiry sits before step 1. The progress control is orientation, not
authority. Server state chooses the active step; browser clicks cannot skip
ahead. Completed steps remain reviewable. On narrow screens, visible copy may
say “Step 3 of 6 — Quote,” while the full ordered list remains available to
assistive technology.

The page hierarchy is:

- one page H1;
- one account/dashboard H2 for “Website assessment”;
- one current-stage H3;
- one restrained global status region;
- one current task card;
- one “What happens next” paragraph; and
- completed quote, invoice, receipt, report, and credit records below the task
  card, newest first, without exposing internal/provider identifiers.

Do not create six unrelated pages, six checkout funnels, a wizard that loses
history, or a second identity system.

### H1 DOM conventions

- Root: section with aria-labelledby and a server-projected data-journey-state.
- Global status: role=status, aria-live=polite, tabindex=-1.
- Blocking validation/error: role=alert adjacent to the relevant field or
  action; do not turn the whole page into an assertive live region.
- Loading: aria-busy=true on the affected panel, stable heading, plain loading
  sentence, and no disappearing page structure.
- Exact facts: semantic dl/dt/dd, never a visual-only grid of unlabeled values.
- Forms: form submit semantics, fieldset/legend for grouped choices, explicit
  label-for relationships, and errors connected with aria-describedby.
- Quote/report panels: tabindex=-1 and programmatic focus only after a
  user-triggered load, acceptance, payment return, blocking failure, or
  successful delivery.
- Buttons: disabled unless both the server-projected action and the separately
  projected runtime capability are true.
- Dynamic lists: stable keys and heading order; no focus theft when background
  polling refreshes.
- No H1 account, quote, payment, report, or credit authority in localStorage,
  sessionStorage, query parameters, hidden inputs, DOM attributes, or cached
  email content.

## 5. Customer-safe H1 projection

The build lane owns final route names and durable schema, but the H1 read model
must project these customer-safe concepts together so the browser does not join
commercial truth itself.

| Projection group | Required safe fields | Forbidden customer fields |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Account | display name, email, organization display name, activated state | password material, activation/recovery token, role internals, other tenants |
| Inquiry | customer-safe reference, submitted facts, received state, linked/unlinked-to-current-account state | owner notes, spam/risk flags, cross-tenant match candidates |
| Site | customer-safe site reference, display name, normalized public URL, customer-stated platform label, origin=external, current state, updated time | tenant IDs, internal supportability/risk, credentials, discovered private facts |
| Assessment | customer-safe reference, presentation state, current step, scope summary, exact delivery date, next-action copy | worker leases, private notes, internal effort/repair units |
| Quote | quote number/reference, version, catalog/terms version labels, scope, exclusions, subtotal/tax/total, currency, expiry, accepted state/time, safe disclosure text | Stripe Price/Product IDs, internal catalog rows, customer/tenant authority IDs |

| Job | safe status, pages/page types in scope, desktop/phone coverage state, customer requests, updated time | owner draft findings before release, internal checklists not meant for customer |

| Credit | $200 amount, USD, available/review-required/used state, same-site/Custom-base eligibility, window end, applied Custom quote/invoice reference when used | ledger IDs, manual override fields, customer-entered amount, transfer/refund action |

Anonymous inquiry fields are exactly:

- name;
- email;
- business or organization;
- public website URL;
- what the customer has noticed, in their own words;
- primary goal; and
- important date, explicitly labeled “context only—not a promised delivery
  date.”

The inquiry must reject or strip password-like secrets and warn before submit:
“Do not include passwords, recovery codes, API keys, card details, health data,
or private customer records.”

Inquiry continuation is one-use and account-bound:

- anonymous submission returns only a customer-safe inquiry reference and an
  opaque continuation proof; it returns no customer, organization, site, or
  owner identifiers;
- after activation or sign-in, the server validates that proof against the
  authenticated session and attaches the inquiry once, under an audit event;
- the browser action says “Continue assessment inquiry [reference]” and never
  chooses the destination organization/customer ID;
- replay returns the already-linked inquiry; an expired/invalid proof says
  “That inquiry link cannot be used. Start a new inquiry or contact Site
  Sourcery,” without revealing another account; and
- the proof is removed from the visible URL/history after capture and is never
  persisted as account authority in browser storage.

External site details are exactly:

- site display name;
- normalized public URL;
- primary goal;
- optional customer-stated platform: WordPress, Squarespace, Wix, Shopify,
  another platform, custom/unknown, or “I do not know”;
- customer-stated approximate public size: 1–10 pages, 11–50, more than 50,
  application-like/unknown;
- customer-stated complexity flags: ecommerce, membership/login, multiple
  locations, regulated subject matter, web application/database, or “I do not
  know”;
- optional pages the customer especially wants considered, labeled as
  suggestions rather than final paid scope; and
- affirmation: “I own this website or am authorized to request a review of its
  public pages.”

No credentials, admin URL, analytics access, DNS access, provider account, file
upload, or private content belongs in H1 site intake.

## 7. Assessment report and finding matrix

### 7.1 Authoritative report structure

The account report uses accessible HTML as its primary authoritative format:

1. Report title and customer/site identity.
2. Quote and assessment references.
3. Delivered date and quoted delivery date.
4. Primary goal supplied by the customer.
5. “What we reviewed” with one to five representative pages/page types.
6. Device coverage: desktop and phone.
7. Plain limits and exclusions.
8. Zero to ten prioritized findings.
9. Recommended order of attention.
10. “What this report does not guarantee.”
11. Next-step choices that create no automatic charge.
12. Exact $200 Custom build credit status and terms.

If no important issue is found inside the bounded review, the report says:

“No prioritized finding was recorded within this bounded review. That is not a
certification that the full website is defect-free, accessible, secure,
compliant, or guaranteed to perform.”

### 7.2 Finding record

Every delivered finding contains:

| Field | Customer presentation | Gate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Order | Finding 1 through Finding 10 | Unique, contiguous, maximum ten |
| Title | Short factual issue | No guaranteed result or sales claim |
| Priority | High, Medium, or Low | Text label, not color alone |
| Practical importance | Why this matters to a visitor or the stated goal | No invented revenue/ranking/accessibility-certification claim |
| Affected scope | References only to named representative pages/page types | Out-of-scope pages require a changed/expanded scope |
| Device | Desktop, Phone, or Both | Both overall device scopes must be accounted for |
| Evidence | At least one screenshot evidence item | Finding cannot be delivered without evidence |
| Observation | What was visibly observed | Separate fact from inference |
| Recommended order | Now, Next, or Later | This is order, not a response-time promise |
| Possible next step | Plain option for later quote discussion | No automatic repair, price, invoice, or charge |

“High” means likely to block or seriously confuse an ordinary visitor in the
reviewed path. “Medium” means a meaningful problem that does not usually block
the whole reviewed path. “Low” means a worthwhile improvement. These labels
rank the bounded observations; they do not certify legal, security, or
accessibility severity.

### 7.3 Evidence record

Each evidence item contains:

- page/page-type reference;
- public URL as reviewed;
- Desktop or Phone;
- captured date;
- visible screenshot;
- concise visible caption;
- useful image alternative that states the observed issue and relevant
  location without repeating the whole caption; and
- optional bounded annotation that does not obscure the underlying evidence.

Do not put the finding only inside image text. A thumbnail opens a larger
view with the same caption and alternative. Evidence must not expose passwords,
tokens, private customer records, unrelated personal information, owner-only
notes, or browser/provider internals.

### 7.4 Owner delivery gate

“Deliver report” is enabled only when:

- invoice payment is confirmed;
- the job belongs to the exact account, organization, and external site asset;
- the accepted quote and delivery date are attached;
- one to five representative scope entries are locked;
- desktop and phone coverage are each recorded for every representative
  page/page-type scope entry;
- finding count is zero through ten;
- every finding has priority, importance, affected scope, observation,
  recommended order, and at least one valid evidence item;

Stable write-command identity rules:

- A safe retry of the same user intent reuses the same idempotency key.
- A materially changed site, quote, or scope requires a new version and new
  semantic command; it cannot reuse stale acceptance.
- A provider-effect uncertainty never creates a fresh payment command.
- Read requests use abort/sequence fencing so a slower old response cannot
  overwrite a newer account/site selection.
- Browser reload reconstructs from server projection; it never restores a
  checked acceptance box or assumes a pending operation succeeded.
- Sign-out aborts reads, clears in-memory H1 selections, and renders no prior
  customer report/credit.
